# Design Library editorial guide — initial calibration

The user explicitly approved the Craftsman sample, including images and layout, as the reference template on 2026-10-25. These standards now govern subsequent articles; each article still needs its own approval.

- Aim for the natural length of the subject. Approximately 1,400–2,500 words is a guideline, not a minimum/maximum requirement. Do not pad or cut useful information simply to hit a number.
- Above roughly 1,800 words, add one or two further photos or infographics to break up the reading. Images must explain the subject.
- Put an “At a glance” summary immediately before “In this article.” Keep it short, useful, and visually scannable, with the main particulars and an important distinction. Use a subject-appropriate diagram when useful. Keep text accessible and selectable; do not flatten essential copy into an image.
- For style articles, include a quick colors-and-materials recognition guide. Use named swatches and specific material descriptions; support them with the article's end-of-article sources. A palette alone cannot identify a style. Separate illustrative digital color families from authenticated building-specific finishes, and historic evidence from contemporary suggestions.
- Adapt the recognition panel to other article categories. Do not invent a house-style palette for a principle, a house type, or a material that does not warrant one.
- Generated imagery may illustrate architectural ideas, but must never be attributed to a named building, documented restoration, or Studio Dee project. A documentary building identity must be verified before naming it. Keep image provenance in article metadata. Reader-facing image captions retain their subject descriptions and end with "Image provided by Studio Dee." without production-method labels. Studio Dee has authorized use of its supplied images; no per-image rights reminder is needed.
- Record exact generation prompts and source output paths. Inspect figures against captions and alt text, and use web-sized derivatives for the preview. Draft assets stay outside publicly served static directories.
- Verify historical claims with linked institutional/primary sources and retain claim-to-source mappings in the article data for editorial review. Display sources only in the end-of-article "Sources & further reading" list; do not show citations within the summary, recognition guide, or article sections. Present contemporary design advice as editorial analysis.
- One restrained closing CTA links to the existing `/start-a-project` form.
- Review only one article at a time. Direction approval, revised content approval, and publication authorization are separate decisions.

## Efficient article review

For each article, verify factual claims against sources, inspect every image at its displayed size and check captions against visible content, review copy and citations, and open the local preview at representative desktop and mobile widths. Run a focused content/visibility check after edits. This is the article acceptance pass; do not repeat the full site build, five-width browser and accessibility matrix, or hash inventory after routine copy/image revisions. Run deeper checks promptly when shared components, routes, image handling, or release policy changes. Before publication, complete a single full production build, typecheck, tests, accessibility/responsive sweep, asset-integrity check, and production access/sitemap audit for the selected release set.
