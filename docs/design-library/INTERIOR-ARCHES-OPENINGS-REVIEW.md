## Source check

- The [National Park Service interior preservation brief](https://www.nps.gov/orgs/1739/upload/preservation-brief-18-interiors.pdf) supports considering character-defining spaces and interior details before alteration.
- [Portland permit guidance](https://www.portland.gov/ppd/residential-permitting/do-you-need-permit/residential-permits) explicitly lists cutting or resizing door openings and moving, removing, or adding walls as work needing a building permit.
- [Oregon Building Codes Division](https://www.oregon.gov/bcd/lbdd/Pages/oregon-permits.aspx) says structural alterations require local permits, with project-specific decisions made by the local building department.
- [Jaipur Heritage Conservation and Promotion Regulations](https://environment.rajasthan.gov.in/content/dam/raj/udh/lsgs/lsg-jaipur/Order/order2019/oct/JHCPR2020.pdf) describes varied haveli courtyard forms and multifoil arches on Jaipur facades. The article avoids presenting one form as universal.
- The [National Park Service masonry article](https://www.nps.gov/articles/common-problems-with-brick-masonry.htm) identifies structural problems around openings when arches or lintels deflect or fail.
