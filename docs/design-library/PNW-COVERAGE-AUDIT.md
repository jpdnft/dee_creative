# Pacific Northwest residential style coverage — 2026-10-25

## Scope and conclusion

Quick comparison of the existing 46-title queue against Oregon/Washington preservation guides, local historic surveys, and a supplementary British Columbia check. This identifies recurring residential categories and regional omissions; it is **not a region-wide statistical ranking**. A historic district's proportions cannot be generalized to the whole Northwest. Coastal cities, inland towns, rural areas, and postwar suburbs differ.

The existing queue covers most familiar broad families. The clearest missing dedicated titles are **Northwest Regional Architecture** and **Minimal Traditional Architecture**. **Oregon Rustic Architecture** and **Shed Style Architecture** would make regional coverage more useful. No new titles have been added; user approval is pending.

## Evidence behind the proposed additions

- **Northwest Regional:** Washington DAHP's National Register guidance lists it as a distinct modern-period classification. A DAHP-hosted Colfax survey describes the regional adaptation of modernism using wood/stone, large windows, and sheltering roofs. King County documents a residential example with post-and-beam construction and extensive glazing. [DAHP classification guide](https://dahp.wa.gov/sites/default/files/NRGuide.pdf), [Colfax survey](https://dahp.wa.gov/sites/default/files/compressed-Reconnaissance%20and%20Intensive%20Level%20%20South%20Mill%20Street%20District%20Colfax%20Final-.pdf), [King County preservation awards](https://kingcounty.gov/en/dept/dnrp/about-king-county/about-dnrp/newsroom/2024-news-releases/01-25-historic-preservation-awards).
- **Minimal Traditional:** Seattle's Fremont survey identifies it among popular late-1930s/postwar residential designs, alongside ranch, Cape Cod, and mid-century modern. Tacoma identifies it in residential historic districts as well. This is a useful gap in an inventory otherwise focused on more recognizable high-style houses. [Seattle Fremont survey](https://www.seattle.gov/Documents/Departments/Neighborhoods/HistoricPreservation/HistoricResourcesSurvey/context-fremont.pdf), [Tacoma districts](https://tacoma.gov/government/departments/planning-and-development-services/historic-preservation/tacomas-historic-districts-landmarks/).
- **Oregon Rustic:** Clackamas County identifies a locally significant tradition using logs, shingles or rough siding and stone, particularly around Mount Hood. Its presence is regionally important without establishing overall numerical prevalence. [Clackamas County guide](https://www.clackamas.us/planning/historicclackamas.html).
- **Shed:** DAHP's guide describes the style's adoption for Northwest living and illustrates residential examples across Washington. Its angular volumes and shed-roof composition merit explicit treatment rather than being lost in a broad modern essay. [DAHP Shed guide](https://dahp.wa.gov/historic-preservation/historic-buildings/architectural-style-guide/shed).

## Existing-title coverage notes

Portland's Irvington National Register nomination demonstrates the local importance of bungalows, Craftsman, Foursquare, Colonial Revival, and English Cottage. It is a neighborhood inventory, not a Portland-wide ranking. Historic Wallingford's local guide similarly supports Craftsman, Queen Anne, Colonial/Dutch Colonial, Tudor, Shingle, and Seattle Box coverage. [Irvington nomination](https://www.portland.gov/sites/default/files/2020-05/nr_noms_10000850.pdf), [Wallingford guide](https://www.historicwallingford.org/learn/architecture-styles/).

For a wider inventory later, French Eclectic/Norman, Classical Revival, and Streamline Moderne deserve consideration. The current Art Deco title does not automatically cover Moderne. These are secondary editorial candidates in this quick audit, not demonstrated “most common” regional omissions. Washington's style guide identifies the wider range. [DAHP style guide](https://dahp.wa.gov/historic-preservation/historic-buildings/architectural-style-guide).

## British Columbia supplement

West Vancouver's municipal guide identifies **West Coast Style / West Coast Modern** as a distinct residential tradition. A Northwest Regional article should compare the neighboring traditions explicitly rather than claim they are identical. If Canadian coverage becomes a major focus, a separate West Coast Modern article would be justified. [District of West Vancouver](https://westvancouver.ca/arts-culture/heritage/west-coast-style).

Vancouver Heritage Foundation's inventory also identifies the **Vancouver Special**, alongside West Coast Modern, rancher, split-level, and older revival styles. Vancouver Special would be a logical optional addition under **House Types & Forms**, not a reason to put every local house form under Architectural Styles. [VHF house-style inventory](https://www.vancouverheritagefoundation.org/discover-heritage/vancouver-heritage-house-styles-hub/house-styles-by-name-and-era/).

## Proposed decision

Approve four additions: Northwest Regional Architecture (`northwest-regional`), Minimal Traditional Architecture (`minimal-traditional`), Oregon Rustic Architecture (`oregon-rustic`), and Shed Style Architecture (`shed-style`). That would change Architectural Styles from 10 to 14 entries and the overall queue from 46 to 50. Until approval, the queue inventory and ordering remain unchanged.
