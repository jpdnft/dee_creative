## Sources and editorial notes

The [City of Cincinnati style guide](https://www.cincinnati-oh.gov/planning/historic-conservation/major-architectural-styles/tudor-revival-1910-to-1940/) supports the American period and characteristic forms. [Oregon's Tarpley House record](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=v.dsp_siteSummary&resultDisplay=63977), [the Oregon Encyclopedia's Autzen House entry](https://www.oregonencyclopedia.org/articles/autzen_house/), and [Oregon's Peacock Lane record](https://heritagedata.prd.state.or.us/historic/index.cfm?do=v.dsp_siteSummary&resultDisplay=676754) support the named Portland examples. [The Royal Family](https://www.royal.uk/english-monarchs-ad-400-1603) supports the Tudor dynasty dates. [National Park Service rehabilitation guidance](https://www.nps.gov/articles/000/guidelines-rehabilitating-historic-buildings.htm) supports the preservation framing; renovation advice is editorial synthesis, not site-specific construction guidance.

The article excludes planned related titles until those routes exist. Its closing CTA uses the existing `/start-a-project` destination. The illustrative swatches are explicitly separated from authenticated historic finishes. Final editorial review should focus on the copy, figure pairings, infographic readability, and any desired emphasis on Tudor cottage versus manor examples.
